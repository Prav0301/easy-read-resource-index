# Topic Taxonomy for Easy Read Resources

This taxonomy provides a clinically useful structure for organising Easy Read resources in this repository. It is designed for UK health, social care, SEND, neurodevelopmental, learning disability, and CAMHS contexts.

It should be used alongside:

- `SOURCES_REGISTER.csv` for source tracking.
- `DOWNLOADS/` topic folders for local copies or placeholders.
- Future metadata fields such as audience, age group, resource type, review date, and CAMHS relevance.

This taxonomy is not a clinical guideline. Resources should still be checked for source credibility, publication date, accessibility quality, cultural appropriateness, and local service fit before use.

---

## Core metadata fields

When adding or reviewing a resource, record these fields where possible:

| Field | Suggested values |
|---|---|
| Topic category | CAMHS; Mental Health; Autism; ADHD; Learning Disability; Medication; Consent and Capacity; Safeguarding; SEND; NHS Access; Parent/Carer; Young People; Inpatient CAMHS |
| Secondary topics | Anxiety; depression; self-harm; crisis; autism; ADHD; learning disability; sleep; medication; rights; appointments; discharge; school; family support |
| Resource type | Leaflet; booklet; poster; video; checklist; social story; appointment guide; medication information; rights information; crisis plan; workbook |
| Format | Easy Read PDF; accessible HTML; plain English; symbol-supported; pictorial guide; audio/video |
| Age group | Under 5; 5-11; 12-15; 16-17; 18-25 transition; adults; all ages |
| Intended audience | Young person; parent/carer; family; clinician; school/SEND professional; social care professional; inpatient staff; community CAMHS staff |
| NHS relevance | Direct NHS care; NHS access/navigation; NHS policy/rights; useful but non-NHS; not NHS-specific |
| CAMHS relevance | Yes; No; Potentially; Indirect |
| Review priority | High; Medium; Low |
| Review notes | Why it is useful, risks/limits, and whether local adaptation is needed |

## CAMHS

**Use for:** resources directly relevant to child and adolescent mental health assessment, treatment, crisis care, neurodevelopmental care, family work, transitions, and service navigation.

**Suggested tags:**

- `CAMHS`
- `child-mental-health`
- `adolescent-mental-health`
- `assessment`
- `therapy`
- `crisis`
- `risk`
- `family-work`
- `transition`
- `school-interface`
- `neurodevelopmental`

**Age groups:** 5-11, 12-15, 16-17, 18-25 transition.

**Intended audiences:** young people, parents/carers, CAMHS clinicians, schools, social care, inpatient and crisis teams.

**Mark CAMHS-relevant when:**

- The resource is written for children, adolescents, families, or professionals supporting them.
- It explains mental health symptoms, assessment, treatment, crisis support, or rights in a way relevant to under-18 services.
- It supports transitions between community CAMHS, crisis services, inpatient CAMHS, schools, social care, or adult services.
- It concerns neurodevelopmental needs where mental health, behaviour, risk, school, or family support are clinically relevant.

## Cross-cutting tag rules

Use multiple tags when a resource spans more than one area. For example:

- An Easy Read leaflet on ADHD medication: `ADHD`, `medication`, `young-people`, `parent-carer`, `CAMHS`.
- A guide to autism reasonable adjustments in hospital: `autism`, `NHS-access`, `reasonable-adjustments`, `learning-disability` if relevant.
- A booklet on self-harm for teenagers: `mental-health`, `self-harm`, `young-people`, `crisis-support`, `CAMHS`.
- A guide to EHCPs for families: `SEND`, `parent-carer`, `school-interface`, `CAMHS` if mental health/neurodevelopmental interface is prominent.

## CAMHS relevance decision rule

Mark a resource as `CAMHS_Relevant = Yes` when at least one of the following applies:

1. It is explicitly written for children, adolescents, parents/carers, or services supporting under-18 mental health.
2. It explains a mental health, neurodevelopmental, medication, risk, safeguarding, consent, SEND, or inpatient issue commonly encountered in CAMHS.
3. It supports communication with young people or families in CAMHS assessment, treatment, crisis planning, discharge planning, or transition.
4. It helps staff make reasonable adjustments for autistic young people, young people with learning disability, or young people with communication needs.
5. It is relevant to multi-agency CAMHS interfaces such as schools, SEND, social care, crisis services, inpatient units, paediatrics, or adult mental health transitions.

Mark as `CAMHS_Relevant = Potentially` when the resource is not child-specific but may be useful after clinical review or adaptation.

Mark as `CAMHS_Relevant = No` when the resource is adult-only, unrelated to mental health/neurodevelopment/SEND/safeguarding/NHS access, or unsuitable for children and families.

## Suggested review priorities

**High priority:**

- Direct CAMHS, inpatient CAMHS, crisis, medication, safeguarding, consent/capacity, autism, ADHD, and learning disability resources.
- Resources from NHS, NICE, GOV.UK, CQC, Royal Colleges, local authorities, or major UK charities.
- Resources likely to be used directly with young people or parents/carers.

**Medium priority:**

- General mental health, SEND, parent/carer, NHS access, and transition resources.
- Resources needing adaptation or local pathway context before use.

**Low priority:**

- Non-clinical resources, general awareness material, or resources not clearly Easy Read but useful for background mapping.

---

## Quality and safety notes

Before recommending or using any resource clinically, check:

- Whether it is genuinely Easy Read rather than only plain English.
- Whether images, symbols, and wording are respectful, non-stigmatising, and age-appropriate.
- Whether it reflects current UK law, NHS pathways, safeguarding standards, and medication guidance.
- Whether source, publication date, and review date are clear.
- Whether the resource needs local adaptation before use in a specific NHS trust, CAMHS team, or inpatient unit.
- Whether the resource is suitable for the individual young person’s developmental level, language, culture, communication needs, and risk context.
